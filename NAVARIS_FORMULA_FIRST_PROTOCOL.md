# NAVARIS Formula-First Analysis Protocol
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-10-28

## Mandatory sequence
For every analytical, financial, operational, strategic, investment, project, KPI, marketing-performance, quality, risk, or business-administration task:

1. **Define the decision/question** and unit of analysis.
2. **State the governing formula/model BEFORE applying it.** If no valid universal formula exists, explicitly state the selected framework, operational definition, and why it fits.
3. **Define every variable**, units, time period, inclusion/exclusion rules, and data source.
4. **Separate evidence from assumptions.** Mark missing inputs as unknown; never invent values.
5. **Apply the formula** only after the inputs and assumptions are visible.
6. **Show calculation steps** and intermediate values where useful.
7. **Interpret the result**, including limits, sensitivity, uncertainty, and risk of misleading comparisons.
8. **Recommend an action** linked to the result, with an owner, deadline, and measurable outcome where applicable.
9. **Cite the source** for non-trivial formulas or models and distinguish established formulas from proprietary/user-supplied models.
10. **Validate the output** with a check, benchmark, second calculation, or independent source when feasible.

## Required response format
- Decision / question
- Formula or model (shown first)
- Variable definitions and units
- Inputs + source / evidence status
- Calculation
- Interpretation and limitations
- Decision / next action
- Sources and validation

## Example: operating cost reduction
Formula (before application):
\[
\text{Cost Reduction \%} = \frac{\text{Baseline Cost} - \text{New Cost}}{\text{Baseline Cost}} \times 100\%
\]
Variables: Baseline Cost and New Cost must cover the same scope, volume, period, service level, currency and cost categories.
Do not claim a 22% saving unless comparable source-backed values produce 22%.

## Missing or disputed formulas
Do not guess a proprietary formula or label a heuristic as a scientific law. State that the exact formula is unavailable, then either request the source or present a clearly labelled standard alternative with its assumptions.

## Scientific integrity
Use authoritative textbooks, peer-reviewed research, standards bodies, official datasets and primary documents where available. Check edition, date, authorship, methods, reproducibility, limitations and licensing. A file's presence in a Library is not proof of scientific validity or completeness. Do not reproduce copyrighted books or paywalled articles wholesale; use lawful access, citation, summaries and user-owned materials.
