## Service boundary
Clearly distinguish:
1. Freight forwarder: arranges/co-ordinates carriage and related logistics.
2. Shipping agent: represents a vessel owner/operator at a port call; may require a separate maritime license.
3. Customs broker/clearance: performs regulated clearance only under the relevant authorization.
4. Carrier: physically transports cargo.
5. Chartering broker/shipbroker: matches charterers and shipowners; tanker fixtures require specialist authority, KYC/sanctions/AML and legal review.
6. Port/terminal operator: controls terminal services and access.
Do not imply NAVIS can provide regulated activity merely because a partner can. Verify the applicable Egyptian licensing, registration, tax, insurance, liability, and contract requirements with official authorities and qualified counsel before trading in its own name.

## First-stage asset-light offer
Start by selling a narrowly scoped, low-capital service only after customer validation:
- Compare freight/forwarder quotations and normalize exclusions.
- Coordinate booking and milestones through qualified licensed partners.
- Document readiness and shipment status tracking.
- Exception escalation and post-shipment cost report.
- Supplier sourcing only when linked to a verified shipment or RFQ.
Do not purchase trucks, vessels, containers, offices, or bespoke software before repeat paid demand is demonstrated.

## Customer and database policy
Prioritize exporters/importers in Alexandria, Borg El Arab, Amreya, Beheira and nearby industrial clusters; then Egypt–Gulf and Egypt–Europe lanes. Use official Egyptian Exporters Portal, Alexandria Chamber of Shipping/member directories, port authority sources, and company-owned websites. Every row needs: stable ID, legal/company name, sector, location, public business contact, source URL, source date, hypothesis of shipping need, verified need status, decision-maker only if public, next action, last verification, partner fit, credit/payment evidence, and cash status.
A company appearing in a directory is not proof it needs forwarding services or can pay. Never fabricate a budget, buyer, shipping volume, need, or contact. Label FACT / COMPANY CLAIM / INFERENCE / VERIFY. De-duplicate before adding.

## Meeting checklist: prospective customs-clearance partner
Before agreement, document:
- First service and target customer
- Roles/responsibilities and who contracts with the customer
- Who performs customs clearance, transport, insurance and claims handling
- Proof of licenses/authorizations and insurance
- Qualified partner list and rate sources
- Customer ownership, data access and confidentiality
- Contributions/capital, expenses, margin/commission, invoicing, payment collection and credit risk
- No-circumvention, non-exclusivity unless justified, exit, disputes and records
- Named customers or anonymized shipment files, with customer permission
No formation expense, exclusivity, client promise, purchase, external quotation or binding commitment without due diligence, written founders' terms and the user's Human Gate.

## Financial equation
Per-shipment contribution = customer fee + disclosed/agreed commission − partner costs − direct operating/transaction costs − evidenced claims/credit costs.
Calculate only with source-backed inputs. Separate revenue from pass-through freight costs. Model deposits/prepayment, partner payment timing, DSO, refunds/claims, currency exposure, taxes, and working capital. CASH COLLECTED = 0 until actual receipt is evidenced.

## Tanker/oil cargo gate
Do not make tanker chartering the initial core offer. Treat as a separate high-risk later lane. Require a documented mandate, verified cargo owner/authority, vessel/owner/manager checks, P&I/ISM evidence, sanctions/AML screening, quantity/quality/ownership proof, charter-party and payment/credit review, and specialist legal advice. No promise to supply a vessel, cargo or route without verified authority and capacity.
